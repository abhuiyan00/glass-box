    Schedule Baseline and Variance

    A baseline is a frozen copy of the plan, taken before tracking starts, that later reality is measured against. Without it a schedule has no memory: the current plan always looks correct, because it has silently absorbed every change. Variance is the difference between the frozen copy and the live one, and it only exists because the copy was kept.

    This is the same discipline as keep-the-superseded-version-in-tree, applied to a schedule rather than to source files — the superseded version is what makes the delta legible.

    #The frozen copy and the delta

    computer-project-management captures the baseline at lab 5–6 and then reports against it:

    Baseline Current Variance
    Duration123 d134 d+11 d
    Work2 640 h2 888 h+248 h
    Cost176 080 zł190 960 zł+14 880 zł

    Finish moves 24 Aug → 8 Sep 2026. The cost variance is not an overspend decision — it is +248 hours at the same hourly rates, because the budget in this plan is 100 % labour. Duration, work and cost are one number expressed three ways.

    #Progress reported three ways, deliberately

    At the June reporting date: 37 % complete by duration, 31 % by work (908 of 2 888 h), 30.7 % of cost incurred. Three different denominators giving three different answers is the point — duration is ahead of work because the completed phases were the short, thinly-staffed ones. A single "percent complete" would hide that.

    The seven zero-day milestones from the work-breakdown-structure carry the per-phase status (Preparatory ✓, Database ✓, Platform late, four pending), so the aggregate percentages are attributable rather than free-floating.

    #What is measured, and what is not

    The repo reports raw deltas only. There is no earned-value analysis anywhere in the readable files — no CPI, no SPI, no EAC, no cost or schedule performance index. That is a real limitation rather than an oversight to fill in: with 30.7 % of cost spent against 37 % of duration elapsed, CPI/SPI would say something the raw deltas do not, and nothing in the repo says it.

    Nor is there a risk register or any probabilistic estimate — see critical-path-method on the absence of PERT.

    #Attribution of the variance

    The +11 days are traced to a named cause rather than left as a number: four tasks extended, most importantly unit testing at +16 days, plus the resequencing recorded as sequence-integration-after-unit-testing. Sixteen days of extension yielding eleven days of slip is float being consumed — see critical-path-method.
