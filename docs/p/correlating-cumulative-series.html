    Correlating Cumulative Series

    Two running totals correlate strongly with each other almost regardless of what they count. The statistic is measuring time, not a relationship — and it is one of the few analytical errors that reliably produces a better-looking result than doing it correctly.

    #Why it happens

    A cumulative series is monotone non-decreasing by construction. Every pair of monotone series is therefore close to a monotone function of a common index, and Pearson's r over them approaches 1 whether or not the underlying daily quantities have anything to do with each other. Cumulative national coffee sales correlate with cumulative reported cases of anything, in any country, over any period.

    The daily series that generated them may be uncorrelated, negatively correlated, or correlated at a lag. None of that survives the cumulation.

    #How it hides

    The failure has no symptom. There is no error, no warning, and no diagnostic that fires — the output is a high correlation coefficient, which is the result the analyst was hoping for. In the coursework version this concept was written from, cumulative case counts were used directly as a predictor and the notebook ended with a causal-sounding conclusion. It looked like the strongest finding in the document.

    The neighbouring version of the same mistake is a lag expressed in row offsets. Row n-2 is "two days ago" only if the series has no gaps; one missing day silently shifts every lag in the dataset. Lags belong on calendar dates.

    #The correction

    Difference before analysing. New cases per day, not cases to date. Then normalise by population, so that area size is not doing the work — cumulative and un-normalised is the combination that measures nothing but how big and how old each area is.

    validation.py enforces the precondition that makes differencing meaningful: the input must be a complete daily series per area with no gaps and no decreases. A cumulative count that goes down is a revision, and revisions have to be handled deliberately rather than differenced into negative "new cases".

    Report both pooled and within-area correlations. The gap between them is where the ecological fallacy lives: a pooled statistic can be dominated entirely by differences between areas while the within-area relationship is flat or reversed.

    #What is left even when it is done right

    An area-day correlation is still a descriptive ecological screen. It cannot support an individual-level or causal claim, because seasonality, policy, mobility, weather, testing access, reporting delay and demographics are all unmodelled and all plausible. The honest output names them. See reproducible-analysis for the other half — a result nobody can regenerate cannot be checked for this either.
