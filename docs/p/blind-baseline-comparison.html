    Blind Baseline Comparison

    Deliberately withhold the domain heuristic from the metaheuristic so that the comparison against that heuristic measures something. A GA seeded with nearest-depot assignments and greedy routes will of course beat greedy — it starts at greedy. What it will not tell you is whether evolutionary search contributed anything.

    So the GA in optimization-methods is built blind on purpose: purely random initial population, no nearest-depot seeding, an i.i.d. depot gene.

    "A deliberately blind GA — purely random initial population, no nearest-depot seeding, an i.i.d. depot gene. This isolates what evolutionary search achieves from scratch and makes the comparison against a strong greedy heuristic a genuine test."

    Optimization Methods/final_project/README.md, 2026-07-10

    #The result is a loss, and it is in the headline table

    The greedy nearest-neighbour heuristic wins 9 of 12 instances on raw distance, and often by a wide margin — Wrocław large 481,089 m for greedy against 1,003,882 m for the tuned GA. Those numbers are the README's own headline table, not an appendix.

    The three exceptions are informative rather than face-saving: the GA wins on Zurich small and Berlin small (as LBBS), i.e. the sparsest instances, where global search has few enough decisions to actually explore the space. The repo states the boundary directly:

    "The from-scratch GA does not beat greedy on raw distance at medium/large scale — by design (no warm start). The exception is the sparsest instances (Zurich small), where global search overtakes the heuristic."

    Optimization Methods/final_project/README.md, 2026-07-10

    #Why this is the methodologically interesting page

    Three things are true at once and the repo lets all three stand:

    1. The GA is worse than a heuristic anyone could write in an afternoon.
    2. That is the expected consequence of a design choice made before the results were known.
    3. Publishing it makes every other claim in the paper more credible, because the experiment demonstrably could have produced an unflattering answer and did.

    A warm-started GA would have produced better numbers and a worse experiment. Choosing the design that can fail is what separates a comparison from a demonstration — and the operator-bake-off rests on the same footing, because a blind GA's ranking over operator types is not contaminated by a seed that already encodes the answer.

    This is the strongest instance of provenance-and-limits-stated-inline in the corpus: the negative result is not merely admitted, it is advertised in the first table a reader sees. The second negative kept on the same principle is revert-depot-mutation-rate-raise.

    #The numbers are externally checked

    The blind GA and greedy totals are re-derivable: tools/verify_reports.py hard-codes the paper's Table 7 values and cross-checks the committed reports against them to ±2 m. Because the expected values live in the checker rather than being read back from the artefact being checked, this is a genuine cross-check rather than a tautology — see generated-audit-artifact.

    The GA is also bit-for-bit reproducible per seed, and infeasibility is priced rather than forbidden (fitness = road distance + 1e6 × overload units), so a run that violates capacity is comparable rather than discarded.
