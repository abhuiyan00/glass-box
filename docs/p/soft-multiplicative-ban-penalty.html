    Soft Multiplicative Ban Penalty

    Decision. Charge a banned leg 3× its real length (LBBSParams::banned_mult = 3.0, applied in BannedDist) instead of adding a flat +1×10⁸ metres.

    Date. 2026-07-10 (committed); diagnosed during the P4 modification round in optimization-methods.

    Rejected alternative. The flat +1e8 penalty that shipped first.

    #The symptom

    The operator-bake-off ranked all 32 operator combinations for the LBBS solver and produced a result that looked like a discovery and was actually a bug: workload_imbalance came out at ~3.70 for every single combination. Thirty-two different search behaviours, one number. That is not what a hard objective looks like; that is what a constant looks like.

    #The diagnosis

    Per-vehicle workload is summed from leg distances scored through BannedDist. A flat +1e8 on the one banned leg made a single vehicle's "distance" ≈1e8 while the other ~14 sat at ~1e5. One outlier across ~15 vehicles forces the coefficient of variation to ≈3.68 regardless of what the GA does. The same 1e8 also swamped the objective — every reported LBBS distance read ≈1.01×10⁸, i.e. the penalty, not the route.

    "the old 1e8 was a single outlier that pinned workload_imbalance at ~3.70 regardless of operators"

    Optimization Methods/final_project/mdvrp/include/lbbs.hpp, 2026-07-10

    #Why multiplicative, and why 3×

    A flat penalty is scale-free in the wrong direction: it is enormous relative to every real distance, so it dominates both the objective and any statistic computed from the scored distances. A multiplicative penalty stays on the real-distance scale by construction — a banned 800 m leg costs 2,400 m, which is a discouragement rather than a prohibition.

    That single change fixed both problems at once, which is why it was preferred to the obvious alternative of keeping the flat penalty and computing the imbalance statistic from unpenalised distances:

    "Re-tuning the penalty to a proportionate 3× removes the outlier, so both the reported fitness and the imbalance fall back onto the real-distance scale — a single change serving both goals."

    Optimization Methods/final_project/output/experiments/lbbs/tuned/mod_note.md, 2026-07-10

    The ban concept survives intact — the longest edge is still discouraged, just proportionately instead of catastrophically.

    #Result

    On the four large instances, best-of-10: fitness moves from ≈1.013×10⁸ to real values around 1.0M, and imbalance drops from ≈3.70 to 0.200–0.318 — a 90–95% reduction. All 12 instances remain feasible. Numbers and the trade-off they represent are on workload-balance-vs-distance.

    #The constraint that shaped it

    The penalty could not simply be handed to the GA. Dist has no virtual operator() and distance.hpp was held fixed — a deliberate consequence of the performance design in lazy-dijkstra-distance-cache:

    "Cannot feed BannedDist into the GA (Dist has no virtual operator() and distance.hpp must not change)."

    Optimization Methods/final_project/mdvrp/include/lbbs.hpp, 2026-07-10

    So in the two-phase design, phase 2 explores with real distances and the ban is applied as soft scoring only in the final decode. The decision is therefore as much about where the penalty could be applied as about its size.

    #Why it is on the wiki

    This is a metric artefact caught by an experiment that had no reason to catch it. The bake-off was built to rank operators; the flat line across all 32 cells was the evidence that the metric was broken. Running a factorial design and looking at the variance rather than only the winner is what made it visible — and the repo published the before/after rather than quietly shipping the fixed numbers, which is provenance-and-limits-stated-inline.
