    Workload Balance Vs Distance

    Minimising total fleet distance and sharing work evenly across vehicles are different objectives, and optimising the first alone reliably produces a fleet where one vehicle does a great deal and others do very little. A distance-optimal plan has no reason to care that vehicle 3 drives 40 km and vehicle 11 drives 4 km — the sum is what is scored.

    multi-depot-vehicle-routing in optimization-methods takes this on directly and pays a measurable price for fairness.

    #⚠ LBBS is the user's own coinage

    The method is called LBBS — Large Banned Balance Split. This name is not a literature term. It is the user's own invented method name, confirmed in interview:

    "A teammate's suggested value; LBBS is my own coinage."

    interview, 2026-07-28

    Anyone citing "LBBS" as an established technique is citing this repository. Recorded here explicitly so the name is never propagated as standard.

    #The two levers

    1. Longest-edge ban. The longest used leg in a solution is put on a ban list and charged a penalty, which pushes the search away from the single long haul that creates the imbalance. Only the top N longest edges are banned (banned_top_n = 1 by default) — the ban is a nudge on one leg, not a constraint set.

    2. LPT trip→vehicle assignment. Once trips exist, they are assigned to vehicles by longest-processing-time first rather than round-robin. LPT is the classic greedy makespan heuristic and it is doing the same job here: put the big items down first so the small ones fill the gaps.

    Two designs of this exist side by side and both are kept: two-phase (lbbs2 — a blind GA finds the ban, then a second GA runs with that ban fixed) and single-stage integrated, where the ban list grows adaptively inside one GA run every lbbs_update_interval generations. Neither is deleted in favour of the other — see multiple-solutions-side-by-side and keep-the-superseded-version-in-tree.

    #The trade, measured

    After the penalty was made proportionate (soft-multiplicative-ban-penalty), per-vehicle imbalance — the coefficient of variation of vehicle workload — drops by roughly 90–95%:

    City (large) Imbalance before Imbalance after
    berlin3.6980.318
    budapest3.7040.203
    wroclaw3.7020.200
    zurich3.7020.261

    The distance cost is small: across the 12 instances lbbs_tuned sits within a few percent of the tuned blind GA, sometimes better (Zurich large 960,974 vs 971,895; Berlin small 209,231 vs 230,170), sometimes worse. Neither beats greedy on distance at scale, which is the point of blind-baseline-comparison and not a failure of the balance objective.

    The honest reading: the "before" imbalance of 3.70 was largely a scoring artefact, so the ~90% figure measures fixing a measurement bug at least as much as it measures balancing a fleet. The repo says this itself rather than reporting the improvement bare — the imbalance was "pinned at ~3.70 for all 32 operator combos", which is the signature of an artefact, not of a hard problem.

    #Why the trade is worth naming

    Distance is the objective that is easy to write down; balance is the one a dispatcher actually complains about. Any fleet objective that is a pure sum is silently indifferent to distribution, and you only find out when the plan meets people. Recording it as a trade rather than as an improvement keeps both halves visible: roughly a few percent of distance bought a fleet where no vehicle carries five times its neighbour's load.
