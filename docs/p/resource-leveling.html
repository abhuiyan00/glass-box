    Resource Leveling

    Resolving over-allocation by moving tasks in time rather than by adding capacity. A schedule built from dependencies alone assumes infinite parallelism; the moment finite people are assigned to it, tasks that used to overlap must be pushed apart. Leveling is the act of doing that, and its price is always duration.

    In computer-project-management the price is stated exactly:

    "The extra 11 days come from resolving over-allocation: once a resource can only do one"

    Computer Project Management/lab3-4_Resource_Assignment/README.md, 2026-07-10
    Lab 2 — dependencies only Lab 3–4 — resourced and leveled
    Tasks3838
    Duration110 d121 d
    Finish5 Aug 202620 Aug 2026

    Same 38 tasks, same dependency network, +11 days. Nothing about the work changed — only the assumption that people can be in two places at once.

    #The two-step that makes it visible

    The lab structure separates defining resources from assigning them, and that separation is what makes the cost measurable. Lab 2 creates a 12-item pool — 9 work resources, 2 cost resources, 1 material resource — and assigns none of it. Lab 3–4 assigns all nine people, MS Project flags the over-allocations, and leveling clears them. Had both happened in one step, the 11 days would have been invisible inside a single number.

    Assignments follow role bands rather than individuals: analysis and management, build (often paired — Design shipment tracking database takes Backend Developer + Database Admin), quality and release, handover, and two all-hands tasks.

    #Leveling vs the alternatives

    Response to over-allocation Effect Taken here
    Level — delay or sequence the conflicting tasksduration grows, cost flatyes, +11 d
    Smooth — move only tasks with float, protect the finish dateduration flat, less slacknot distinguished from leveling anywhere in the repo
    Add a resourceduration may shrink, cost growsnot taken — and the repo's own seminar argues against it via Brooks' Law, see project-environment-factors
    Overtime / raise availabilityduration flat, cost growsnot considered in any readable file

    Leveling appears twice in this plan under two different names: once here, and again in lab 5–6 where a Backend Developer conflict is resolved by re-linking two tasks — a manual leveling action recorded as a dependency decision, sequence-integration-after-unit-testing.
