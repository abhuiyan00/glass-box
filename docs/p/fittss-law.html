    Fitts's Law

    Paul Fitts, 1954. The time to point at a target depends on how far away it is and how big it is, logarithmically:

    MT = a + b · log₂(2D / W)

    D is the distance to the target, W its width along the axis of movement, and a and b are fitted constants for the device and the person.

    #Two consequences worth more than the formula

    Doubling the width buys the same as halving the distance. Both change D/W by a factor of two, so both save b milliseconds. Layout arguments about proximity and arguments about size are the same argument, and they can be traded against each other quantitatively.

    A target at a screen edge is effectively infinitely wide. The pointer cannot overshoot past the edge, so W in the direction of travel is unbounded and the log term collapses. That is why menu bars sit flush against edges and corners, and why a one-pixel gap below a bar destroys the advantage completely — the target stops being an edge target and becomes an ordinary one.

    The edge case is the design. This is the same shape of reasoning as the boundary conditions that carry the weight in computer-arithmetic-and-number-representation — the p's complement of zero, the most negative two's-complement value, the smallest subnormal. Interesting behaviour lives at the limits of the range.

    #Why this is in the wiki and the Gestalt principles are not

    Fitts's Law makes a numeric prediction about a specific interface, so it can be wrong about that interface, and being wrong is checkable. "Apply the principle of closure" cannot be wrong about anything.

    Both belong in a human-computer interaction course and only one belongs in a test suite. Knowing which is which is the transferable skill, and it generalises well past interface design: the same distinction separates counted-complexity-assertions from a docstring claiming an algorithm is fast.

    #The limit of the law

    It predicts pointing time. It says nothing about whether the user knew which target to point at, whether the target was the right one to offer, or whether the task should have existed. A layout optimised purely on Fitts's Law can be measurably fast and useless.

    That boundary is why the concept sits at confidence: medium and why no repository was built from it: a formula is not an artefact, and the assessed half of the course — heuristic evaluation — is a rubric applied by a person, which rebuilds into a form rather than a program.

    #Source

    The archive's HCI material teaches it with the equation rather than as a slogan, and works the edge-target argument through real sites. Recorded in pwr-subject-notes; the folder produced no repository and the reason is written down there rather than left implicit.
