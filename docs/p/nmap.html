    Nmap

    The network mapper: send crafted packets to a target and characterise it from the responses. Host discovery, port state, service and version identification, OS fingerprinting, traceroute, and a scripting engine (NSE) on top. It carries lab 11 of cybersecurity-engineering-laboratories and opens lab 12.

    Unlike a naive port scanner it accounts for network conditions — latency, congestion, interference — while it runs, which is what makes its ambiguous results meaningful rather than noise.

    #Six port states, and why three of them are the interesting ones

    State Meaning
    openan application is accepting connections
    closedthe port is reachable, nothing is listening
    filtereda packet filter is blocking the probe
    unfilteredreachable, but open/closed cannot be determined
    `open\filtered`no response — could be either
    `closed\filtered`ambiguous under the scan type used

    filtered and the two ambiguous states are findings about the defence, not about the service. They tell you a filter exists and, by comparing across scan types, roughly what it does. A tool that only reported open/closed would discard that information.

    #The scan-type family

    Different TCP flag combinations elicit different responses from different stacks and filters — connect, SYN (half-open), null, FIN, Xmas/Maimon, ACK, window, idle. Plus UDP, which is slow and unreliable by nature because a silent port and a filtered port look identical.

    Running several is the technique. Any one scan gives a partial picture; the differences between their answers characterise the firewall. That is the general shape of network-reconnaissance: measurements that only mean something in comparison.

    Layered on top: -sV service and version detection, -O OS fingerprinting, -T0..-T5 timing templates, NSE scripts, and fragmentation/decoy/MTU options for firewall and IDS evasion. The MTU option must be a multiple of eight or Nmap refuses — one of the small details the lab records.

    #Speed against stealth

    The lab states the trade directly: parameters exist that make a scan practically undetectable but take weeks, and parameters that return results in seconds while generating traffic that is trivial to notice. There is no default that is correct for both, and choosing is a decision about what you are willing to spend.

    #Version detection is the pivot to everything after

    Lab 12's chain begins with a full-range -A scan to identify services and versions, which then feeds CVE lookup and exploitation with metasploit. The defensive reading is the same fact from the other side: every banner an active scan reads is a banner an attacker can look up.

    The lab also records the scan failing to return a version for a service that was demonstrably running, and a port not appearing where expected — honest reporting of a tool returning an incomplete answer, which is the normal case rather than the exception. Zenmap, the GUI front end, would not install.

    #Legality

    The lab material states that scanning is regulated differently in different jurisdictions and that in some, running it against infrastructure you do not own is an offence regardless of intent. All work here was 2022 coursework against lab targets provided for the purpose.
