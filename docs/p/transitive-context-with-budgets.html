    Transitive Context with Budgets

    A subtask in a plan should see the finished work it depends on. Chasing that idea honestly produces three separate questions, and answering them one at a time is the whole design in agent-orchestrator.

    #1. How much of the graph — everything upstream, ordered by distance

    Not just the direct dependencies: the transitive ancestors, nearest first, cycle-safe. A subtask that depends on B which depended on A was written on the assumption that A happened. But direct and indirect are not equal, so they are not treated equally — direct dependencies are content, ancestors are background.

    #2. How much text — a budget per relation, and a cap on the whole

    CONTEXT_DIRECT_BUDGET    = 24000   # chars per subtask this one directly depends on
CONTEXT_ANCESTOR_BUDGET  =  6000   # chars per indirect ancestor (background only)
CONTEXT_TOTAL_BUDGET     = 60000   # chars for the whole shared-context block
CONTEXT_MAX_ANCESTORS    =     6   # beyond this, the oldest background is named only

    Direct dependencies are served first and at full budget; background gets what is left, and is dropped rather than starved when under 400 characters remain. Without a total cap, a wide plan composes a prompt that costs more than the work.

    #3. Which part of the text — the middle goes, and says so

    The obvious trim is the tail, and it is the wrong one:

    "Head and tail are what carry meaning in an agent's answer: what it set out to do and what it concluded. The middle is the working."

    vs-llm-orchestrator/agent-orchestrator/backend/graph.py, 2026-08-07

    So _clip keeps 70% head and the rest tail, and splices in a marker naming the exact number of characters removed, plus an instruction not to guess what was there — read the files it describes, or say you could not see it. Silent truncation is the failure mode being designed against: a model handed a mutilated document with no seam confabulates the join, and the confabulation is indetectable downstream. A stated gap is recoverable; an unstated one is not.

    The same clip runs again at the merge step, there with an equal share per subtask rather than a per-relation budget — the reader is different, so the policy is.

    #Why this is a policy and not a heuristic

    Every number above is arbitrary in magnitude and deliberate in shape: direct > indirect, bounded total, bounded fan-in, loss disclosed. Those four properties survive any retuning of the constants, which is what makes it something to write down rather than a tuned parameter.
