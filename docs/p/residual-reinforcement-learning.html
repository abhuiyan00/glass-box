    Residual Reinforcement Learning

    A deterministic controller does the driving; the learned policy only outputs a bounded correction on top of it. The agent never has to rediscover behaviour that classical control already gets right — it spends its whole sample budget on the part the controller gets wrong.

    The trade is explicit. You give up the possibility of the policy discovering a strategy the baseline forecloses, and you inherit every bias in the baseline. What you buy is that a random policy at initialisation still drives roughly correctly, so early episodes produce useful data instead of immediate termination.

    #As implemented

    In the a.bhuiyan tree of it-applications-in-business-and-commerce:

    • _baseline_action is a full hand-written driver — heading and cross-track error terms, a curvature feed-forward, a per-distance braking envelope derived from the minimum-curvature-racing-line speed profile, grade-aware hill logic, and an rpm gearbox with hysteresis and a hold timer (see data-driven-gearing-over-hand-tuned-rpm-bands).
    • The proximal-policy-optimisation policy's output is blended in at ±0.5 — a hard cap on how far the agent can deviate, exposed as --residual-scale.
    • _launch_warmup brings the car to roughly 90 km/h under baseline control before the agent gets the wheel, so no episode is spent learning to start.
    • _apply_steer_constraints adds a speed-scaled clip and a rate limit on top, so even a saturated residual cannot produce a physically absurd steering command.
    • The observation is a 33-dimensional tracking-error state built by flatten_racingline_observation — deviations from the precomputed line, not raw sensors. The policy sees "how wrong is the baseline right now", which is the only thing it can act on.

    --no-assist disables the whole arrangement, which is how the ablation is expressed.

    #The self-learning corner memory

    A second, non-gradient learning loop sits beside the policy. When the car goes off track, the environment records the arc-length coordinate, slows that corner's target speed, and propagates the same reduction to curvature look-alikes elsewhere on the track. The result persists to a .npy that the environment auto-loads, and analyze_telemetry.py can rebuild it offline from logged laps.

    • TODO: no convergence criterion or stopping rule for the corner memory is stated anywhere in the repo, and nothing documents what prevents oscillation between over- and under-slowing a corner. confidence: low on its stability. Do not assert it converges.

    #Outcome, stated honestly by the source

    This branch's own README records that it did not fully solve the S-curve and that the car did not finish a clean full lap in the earlier runs. The top-level project/README.md softens the same result to "reduced but didn't eliminate S-curve errors" — one of the drift instances catalogued on documentation-drift. The branch that shipped as the headline result is the other one; see ship-two-branches-not-all-eight.
