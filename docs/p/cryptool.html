    CrypTool

    A free cryptography teaching application developed by German universities, built to make cryptographic mechanisms visible rather than merely available. Version 1 carries labs 01–06 of cybersecurity-engineering-laboratories — the entire cryptography half of the course.

    #What it provides

    Menu area Used for
    Encrypt/Decrypt → Symmetric (Classic)Caesar, substitution, Playfair, Vigenère, Hill, ADFGVX, homophonic
    Encrypt/Decrypt → Symmetric (Modern)DES, 3DES, AES, RC4
    Analysis → Tools for analysisentropy, ASCII frequency histograms, autocorrelation, periodicity, n-grams, floating frequency
    Analysis → Symmetric (Classic) → Ciphertext-onlyautomatic cipher-breaking
    Analysis → Asymmetricprime generation, RSA factorisation, lattice attacks on RSA
    Indiv. Procedures → RSA Cryptosystemkeygen, factorisation of a supplied modulus
    DemonstrationsDiffie–Hellman, hash collisions, digital signature workflow

    The analysis menu is why the tool is worth a page: it turns cryptanalysis-by-measurement from an idea into a two-click experiment. Encrypt a text, measure its entropy, plot its histogram, compare against the plaintext — the comparison is the lesson, and it happens in seconds.

    #What it demonstrates well

    • Entropy and histograms side by side across six languages and every classical cipher, producing the ranking on cryptanalysis-by-measurement.
    • Autocorrelation recovering a key length for polyalphabetic ciphers — a parameter recovered by measurement, not by guessing.
    • A hash collision breaking a signature, which is the clearest possible statement of why collision resistance is the load-bearing hash property. See digital-signatures.
    • The Diffie–Hellman exchange as a walkthrough, with each participant's values shown at each step.

    #Its limits, as the labs found them

    • Only CBC and ECB among block cipher modes. The lab reached OFB and CFB through an online encryption service instead — see block-cipher-modes. That is a real gap in a tool being used to teach modes of operation, and pushing key material through a third-party web page to fill it is a practice hazard whatever the pedagogy.
    • Stability. The lab-02 report records the application crashing when asked for a histogram after homophonic encryption.
    • Automatic breaking is not reliable on modern ciphers, which is the correct result: the lab-05 brute-force exercises against AES recovered a direction rather than a key, and the report says so.
    • CrypTool 1 is the oldest of the family (CrypTool 2, JCrypTool and CrypTool-Online all exist). TODO: why version 1 was used is not recorded in the repository. Do not infer a preference — confidence: low on the motivation, confidence: high on the fact.

    #Why a teaching tool earns a wiki page

    Because the alternative shapes the learning. Writing the same experiments against OpenSSL or a Python library gives real artefacts and no visualisation; CrypTool gives visualisation and leaves nothing reviewable behind — every result is a screenshot in a report rather than a script someone could re-run. That trade shows up directly in this repository: the crypto labs are PDFs of observations, while the one thing that is reproducible is a 50-line Python fixture generator. See generate-fixtures-not-commit and, for the contrast, openssl.
