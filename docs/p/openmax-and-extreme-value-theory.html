    OpenMax and Extreme Value Theory

    The statistical family's answer to open-set-recognition: instead of thresholding a confidence score by eye, model the tail of the score distribution — the rare, extreme region where outliers live — and use that model to say how likely a sample is to be unknown.

    Extreme Value Theory (EVT) is the general tool. Ordinary statistics fits the bulk of a distribution; EVT fits its extremes, which is exactly the region a rejection decision depends on and the region a Gaussian fit gets worst.

    #What OpenMax does

    OpenMax (Bendale & Boult, CVPR 2016) extends the softmax layer so it can express an unknown class. Three steps, as docs/01 of research-methodologies-open-set-recognition sets them out:

    1. recalibrate the network's penultimate activation vector,
    2. fit a Weibull distribution — an EVT model — to the tail of the per-class activations,
    3. use that fit to estimate the probability the sample belongs to no known class.

    The effect on the output vector is the whole idea in one row:

    Class 1 Class 2 Class 3 Unknown
    Softmax0.300.500.200.00
    OpenMax0.250.400.150.20

    Softmax must distribute all probability across the known classes and therefore cannot represent doubt at all. OpenMax moves part of the mass onto "unknown", so rejection becomes a comparison between numbers rather than an external threshold bolted on afterwards.

    #Why it is the baseline, not the answer

    OpenMax is the method every later paper reports against, and in the benchmarks cited by the study it improves on plain softmax (MSP) only modestly — and on the hardest dataset, TinyImageNet, the two are effectively tied near coin-flip AUROC. The generative route (generative-adversarial-network, then OpenGAN) is where the study finds a large gap, which is why OpenMax appears here as the thing to beat. Those numbers are cited from the source paper, not reproduced — report-cites-benchmarks-rather-than-reproducing.

    It is worth keeping the tension recorded: the Vaze et al. result quoted on open-set-recognition suggests a stronger closed-set classifier alone would also lift the softmax and OpenMax baselines. The study reports both and resolves neither.

    #Placement in the taxonomy

    Threshold and distance methods (traditional) pick a cut with no model of the score distribution. OpenMax/EVT (statistical) model the tail but leave the representation untouched. Metric learning and generative methods (deep) change the representation itself. The three families are answering the same question at three different depths.
