    Project Environment Factors

    The forces surrounding a project that determine whether it succeeds, independent of how good the engineering is. The seminar in computer-project-management (Topic #12, 15 slides) argues the strong form of this: most IT projects fail not from technical shortcomings but from an inability to handle a constantly changing environment.

    #The framework stack it uses

    Frame Distinguishes
    PESTLEthe macro environment — Political, Economical, Sociological, Technological, Legal, Environmental
    EEFs vs OPAsEnterprise Environmental Factors (the climate you are given) vs Organizational Process Assets (the toolbox you inherit)
    Socio-technical modelprojects are driven by people, not by charts and data
    PMBOK vs PRINCE2a descriptive knowledge base vs a prescriptive methodology
    The 4 PsPeople · Process · Politics · Performance
    Brooks' Lawadding people to a late project makes it later

    The EEF/OPA split is the one that does real work: it separates what a project manager can change from what must be absorbed. Everything a plan can control lives in OPAs; PESTLE lives entirely in EEFs.

    #Why this sits next to the scheduling pages

    The seminar and the labs are deliberately opposite halves of the same course, and the repo says so:

    "Slide 12's Process pillar is exactly what the MS Project plan delivers; the other three P's (People, Politics, Performance) are what the seminar argues you cannot schedule your way out of."

    Computer Project Management/seminar/README.md, 2026-07-10

    That is a sharper claim than it first reads. Of the 4 Ps, the entire apparatus of work-breakdown-structure, critical-path-method, resource-leveling and schedule-baseline-and-variance addresses exactly one.

    Brooks' Law is the direct connection. The labs hit a Backend Developer over-allocation and fix it by resequencing rather than by hiring — sequence-integration-after-unit-testing. The seminar supplies the argument for why that is the right instinct: communication overhead grows non-linearly, so the extra head does not buy proportional throughput. Two artefacts in one repo, one supplying the reason for the other's choice, without either cross-referencing it.

    #Toxic environments, named

    Slide 9 names three anti-patterns as things to steer clear of — Death March, Adrenaline Junkies, Template Zombies — and slide 10 turns them into recommendations: embrace change, dismantle toxic cultures, monitor the environment, retrain project managers. Slide 6 argues the related point that "freezing" requirements fails under a dynamic ICT environment, and that conceptual integrity has to be maintained through change rather than by preventing it.
