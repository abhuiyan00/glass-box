    Research Methodologies — Open-Set Recognition

    Despite the course name, this repo does not teach research methods — it applies one. It is a written literature study on open-set-recognition culminating in OpenGAN, produced for the Research Methodologies course (WUST, Electronic Media in Business and Commerce, 2026). 9 files, 31 KB of prose across three markdown documents.

    It is the only two-author repository in the source set. Wuxi Bao and Alimuzzaman Bhuiyan are credited jointly in README.md §9 and in the NOTICE copyright line.

    Status: coursework, finished and frozen at last commit 2026-07-11. "All finished and frozen" (interview, 2026-07-28).

    #The method it actually used

    literature-review-synthesis, not experimentation. No dataset, no code, no sampling frame, no hypothesis test. The design is: frame the problem → map the method families → deep-dive the generative family → spotlight one method → compare on benchmarks taken from the source paper. Quality control is a stated provenance boundary rather than reproduction — report-cites-benchmarks-rather-than-reproducing.

    #Content recorded as concepts

    open-set-recognition (closed-set vs open-set, rejection as a valid answer) · openmax-and-extreme-value-theory (Weibull tail fit redistributing softmax mass) · generative-adversarial-network (the minimax objective, Wasserstein loss as the stability alternative) · literature-review-synthesis (the method itself).

    OpenGAN's two moves are the report's payload: discriminate in feature space rather than pixel space, and select the checkpoint on a small held-out open set. The report is explicit that without the second move performance is "essentially random".

    #Findings

    • Five commits in 16 seconds on 2026-07-11, in publish order — scaffolding + slides, figure, both chapters, the main report, licence + NOTICE. import-finished-work-into-git in its purest form; the work itself has no git trace at all.
    • This is the only repo in the group with no origin/HEAD set — a small tell that the remote was created differently from computer-project-management, physics-exam-prep and privacy-policy-template.
    • The provenance boundary is the repo's strongest engineering habit. NOTICE, the inline "not experiments run for this coursework" note under the results table, and the licence footer all say the same thing three times in three places. See provenance-and-limits-stated-inline.
    • A stated finding the report deliberately keeps in tension with its own conclusion. The report spotlights OpenGAN while also quoting Vaze et al.'s result that "a good closed-set classifier is already a good open-set detector" — an argument that a stronger baseline may close much of the gap. Both claims are left standing; neither is resolved. Recorded rather than tidied, in the spirit of multiple-solutions-side-by-side.
