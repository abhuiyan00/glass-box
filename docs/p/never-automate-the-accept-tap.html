    Never Automate The Accept Tap

    Decision. farely advises; the driver taps. The app may read offers, score them and display a verdict, but it may never dispatch the gesture that accepts or declines a ride.

    Date. 2026-07-02, VISION §7. Restated in SRS NFR-R-3, CONTRIBUTING, and the header comments of the coordinator in both TypeScript and Kotlin.

    #The reason, as written

    "❌ No auto-accept/auto-decline of ride offers. The money decision stays human — Farely advises, the driver taps Accept. Automating that tap raises ban and safety stakes far higher and is out of scope."

    Farley/docs/VISION.md, 2026-07-02

    Two distinct stakes, deliberately not collapsed. Ban risk: an automated accept is the behaviour platform anti-fraud systems are built to detect, and it would put the driver's livelihood on the correctness of a heuristic parser. Safety: the accept happens while driving, and an app that can commit the driver to a journey has taken over a decision with physical consequences.

    The scope line is drawn at the money decision, not at automation in general.

    #What is still automated

    Multi-app "chore" automation was accepted on 2026-07-12 — housekeeping in the other driver apps: online/offline toggles, "stop new requests", trip and marker state. The rule that keeps these apart is that chore controls must be taught by the driver rather than guessed, and the teaching surface is restricted to chore controls only. An Accept button cannot become a taught selector. See taught-selectors-over-guessed-labels.

    #Alternative rejected

    Full autopilot — score, then accept above a threshold. This is the obvious product, and it is what the rest of the architecture would support for free: the parser already reads the offer and the service already holds gesture-dispatch capability. Rejected on the ban and safety grounds above, not on technical difficulty.

    #How the boundary is enforced

    Structurally rather than by convention, which is what distinguishes it from privacy-documented-beside-the-feature's softer guarantees:

    • Coordinator roles are enumerated — online/offline, stopRequests, markers — and no offer action exists in the enum.
    • The classifier's action vocabulary is closed: freeze | note | map-control | none.
    • The instruction is repeated to the model in the anthropic-messages-api system prompt ("Never propose tapping accept"), so a cloud response cannot introduce an action the local code would honour.

    The same restraint governs the sibling invariant — never come between the driver and an identity check:

    "Deliberately excludes verification/selfie terms: identity checks are detected on-device by the coordinator and NEVER routed to cloud vision."

    Farley/apps/android/.../FarelyAccessibilityService.kt, 2026-07-13

    That one is a keyword heuristic and therefore weaker; the Accept boundary is stronger because there is no code path to abuse. Worth keeping the distinction — see opt-in-cloud-fallback.
