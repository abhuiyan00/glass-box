    Data Minimisation

    GDPR Art. 5(1)(c): personal data must be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for the purpose. It is the principle that makes every other privacy obligation cheaper — data never collected needs no legal basis, no retention schedule, no breach notification and no deletion mechanism.

    privacy-policy-template names it and then organises §4 around it:

    "We practise data minimisation — we collect as little as possible."

    privacy-policy/PRIVACY_POLICY.md, 2026-07-10

    #What survives the principle

    Category What it is Source
    Information you provideemail address and message content, only if you choose to writedirectly from you
    Technical / log datastandard server logs from the host — IP, user-agent, pages requested, timestampautomatically, via the hosting provider
    Cookiesnone set by this Service by default—

    Two categories, one of which the site does not choose to collect and cannot switch off — it is a by-product of being hosted at all, which is why it lands on controller-vs-processor rather than on the controller's own conscience. Sensitive categories (government IDs, health, biometric, precise geolocation, racial or ethnic origin, religion, financial account data) are excluded explicitly, with a request not to send them.

    #Why the discipline compounds

    Because collection is near-zero, most of the policy's remaining sections can make absolute rather than hedged statements: no analytics, no advertising, no fingerprinting, no profiling, no automated decision-making with legal effect, no sale or sharing, no "Do Not Sell" mechanism required. Each of those is a claim the document can only make because §4 is short.

    The same relationship works in reverse, and is the standing risk on one-reusable-policy-not-per-project: apply this template to a project that does collect — accounts, a database, a third-party API — and the absolute statements downstream become false without anyone editing them.

    #The interaction with legal bases

    Minimisation and gdpr-legal-bases are not independent. The policy leans on legitimate interests for operating and securing the Service, which is the basis with a balancing test attached; a minimal dataset is what makes that test easy to pass. Consent is reserved for the one thing the visitor initiates — email — where it is unambiguous.

    confidence: high: the principle is named verbatim in the canonical markdown and repeated in the HTML rendering, and the collection table is read directly from both.
