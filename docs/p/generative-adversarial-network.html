    Generative Adversarial Network

    Two networks trained against each other as a two-player game. A generator G turns a random noise vector z into a fake sample; a discriminator D learns to tell real data from those fakes. As D improves, G is pushed to produce fakes good enough to fool it; at equilibrium the generator's samples are ideally indistinguishable from real data.

    #The minimax objective

    docs/02 of research-methodologies-open-set-recognition derives it term by term:

    E_x[ log D(x) ]  +  E_z[ log(1 − D(G(z))) ]
    Symbol Meaning
    D(x)the discriminator's estimate that a real instance is real
    G(z)the generator's output from noise z
    D(G(z))the discriminator's estimate that a fake instance is real

    D maximises this — it wants D(x) near 1 and D(G(z)) near 0. G minimises it — it wants D(G(z)) near 1. The first term scores real samples, the second scores generated ones, and the push-and-pull between them is the entire training signal.

    Training alternates by minibatch stochastic gradient descent: k discriminator ascent steps per generator descent step, with k = 1 the least expensive and most common choice.

    #Instability is the defining practical property

    The chapter flags it before it needs it, which is what makes the later argument work: plain minimax GANs are unstable to train. The named remedy is the Wasserstein loss (WGAN, Arjovsky et al., 2017), which replaces the objective with a smoother one giving more useful gradients.

    That instability is what disqualifies the obvious application to open-set-recognition:

    "| Fix 2 | Reuse a GAN discriminator as the detector — treat “fake” as “unknown.” | GAN training is unstable, so the raw discriminator is unreliable as a detector. |"

    Research Methodologies/docs/02-gans-and-opengan.md, 2026-07-11

    OpenGAN's answer is not to stabilise training but to route around it: discriminate on feature vectors from a frozen classifier rather than pixels, and pick the checkpoint by evaluating against a small held-out open set. The generator's endless supply of fake features is what removes the dependence on a fixed, finite outlier set — the flaw that also sinks the alternative fix of simply training on collected outliers.
