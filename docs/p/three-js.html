    Three.js

    WebGL scene-graph library. Used at ^0.185.1 in stickfps, and there only — it is the single instance of 3D rendering in the source set.

    #How it is used: primitives all the way down

    There is no asset pipeline. No .glb, no textures on disk, no fonts. Environment.tsx builds the entire arena out of box, plane and cylinder geometry in 576 lines; the stickmen are generated procedurally; textures are inline-SVG data URIs. That constraint is deliberate and predates the code — see no-external-assets.

    The practical consequence is that the whole visual identity lives in code that diffs, tests and reviews like code, and that a design change is an edit rather than a re-export. The cost is that everything costs geometry setup time, and that the art ceiling is set by what you can express in primitives.

    Three.js is consumed through react-three-fiber rather than directly — imperative scene.add calls are rare in the client.

    #Three gotchas the build actually hit

    These are worth recording because all three cost a round each and none are obvious from the API:

    • A mesh parented to the camera is invisible unless the camera is itself a descendant of scene. The first-person weapon rig in stickfps attached correctly, rendered nothing, and was return nulled for two rounds before the real cause was found.
    • The default directional-light shadow camera is a ±5 m box. An arena larger than that renders near-black outside it, which reads as "lighting is broken" rather than "the shadow frustum is small".
    • Lighting intensities are unit-sensitive across versions. Values tuned for legacy lighting units produce a scene far darker or brighter than intended after the physically-correct default.

    Lighting broke twice in total: once too dark to read cover by, once near-black from the shadow camera.

    #Vendored knowledge about it

    The repo commits 19 Three.js "skill packs" — 87 files of reference tables and NEVER/ALWAYS rules — alongside the product. That is documented as its own habit in agent-skills-checked-into-the-repo, including the version mismatch between what the packs declare and what the project runs.
