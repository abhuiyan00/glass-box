    Omit the RSA Private-Key Screenshot

    Decision. Do not use the natural illustration for the applied-cryptography lab, because that page prints a private key. Use the signature-verification page instead, and say in the repository that the substitution was made and why.

    Date. 2026-07-25 (the publishing commit), assets/ of cybersecurity-engineering-laboratories.

    #Why

    The lab's most representative page is the terminal where the keypair is generated — it is the step the whole lab is built on, and it is the obvious thing to show. It also prints the key in full:

    "The obvious illustration for lab 7 is not used. The natural screenshot for that lab is the terminal where the RSA keypair is generated — and it prints the full 2048-bit private key. The signature-verification page is used instead."

    Cybersecurity-Engineering-Laboratories/README.md, 2026-07-25

    The reasoning generalises past this one key. The keypair was a throwaway generated for a lab exercise, so on a narrow reading nothing is at risk. The rule applied is stronger and is stated separately for the PKCS#12 case:

    "No private key files are committed. ... a .p12 is a private key container by definition and those do not belong in a public repository regardless of what is inside them."

    Cybersecurity-Engineering-Laboratories/README.md, 2026-07-25

    "Regardless of what is inside them" is the decision. Classify by kind, not by assessed value. A rule that requires judging each key's importance fails the first time the judgement is wrong or hurried, and secret-scanning tools and human readers both key off the shape of the thing, not its provenance.

    #Alternatives rejected

    Option Why not
    Publish it — the key is throwawayrequires the reader to trust a per-item risk assessment; normalises private keys appearing in repositories
    Redact the key region of the screenshotpartial redaction of a dense block reliably misses something, and a cropped terminal is not obviously complete
    Regenerate with a tiny key for the screenshotstill a private key in the image, and now a misleading one
    Omit lab 7 from the illustrations entirelyloses the lab that best represents the defensive half of the course

    #What makes it a decision rather than a preference

    It cost something specific — the second-best illustration for the lab — and it is recorded at the point of use. assets/README.md names the source page of every screenshot and names the omission:

    "Lab 7's key-generation page is deliberately not among these: it prints a full RSA-2048 private key to the terminal."

    Cybersecurity-Engineering-Laboratories/assets/README.md, 2026-07-25

    An omission that is not documented is indistinguishable from an oversight. Writing it down is what converts "there is no keygen screenshot" into evidence of a rule being followed. Compare provenance-and-limits-stated-inline.

    Structurally the same defaulting appears in .gitignore, which excludes *.p12, *.pfx, *.pem, *.key, *.jks, *.keystore and id_rsa* so the rule holds without anyone remembering it — secrets-from-environment-only is the same instinct applied to configuration. See openssl for why key-generation output looks the way it does, and public-key-infrastructure for why the private half must never leave the machine that made it.

    This page deliberately does not describe what the omitted screenshot contained beyond the fact that it was a private key. Reproducing the omission would defeat it.
