    Cybersecurity Engineering Laboratories

    Fourteen laboratories from the Cybersecurity Engineering course at Wrocław University of Science and Technology, carried out in 2022 and imported into git in 2026. The deliverable is written reports: 14 PDFs totalling exactly 182 pages, plus ~20 small input fixtures, 6 rendered screenshot pages and one 50-line stdlib-only Python helper.

    "The course builds in one direction: understand a primitive, break it, then use what survives to defend a system."

    Cybersecurity-Engineering-Laboratories/README.md, 2026-07-25

    Status: coursework, finished and frozen at last commit 2026-07-25. The course is done and nothing is maintained ("All finished and frozen" — interview, 2026-07-28). The abandoned tag on this page refers to the individual exercises the reports themselves record as unfinished (below), not to the repository.

    #Stack

    cryptool 1 carries labs 01–06. openssl with GnuPG and Easy-RSA carries the applied-crypto lab. wireshark captures in labs 08–09, with OpenVPN, SSH and netcat providing the transports. nmap and metasploit carry recon and exploitation; owasp-zap and Burp Suite carry the web labs against OWASP Juice Shop. Everything runs on VirtualBox VMs — kali-linux as the attack host, Ubuntu Server, Metasploitable 2/3 and pfSense as targets.

    Balance: predominantly offensive. Labs 02–06 are cryptanalysis and measurement; 08 and 10–14 are attack. The defensive core is exactly two labs — 07 (PKI, CA, trust) and 09 (VPN/TLS/SSH plus capture analysis).

    #Findings

    • The reports are substantially more incomplete than the README implies. The README calls the 182 pages a record of "what was attempted, what the tools returned, and what the result means"; the reports themselves record repeated failures: the lab-06 Diffie–Hellman exchange over OpenSSL ("did not work", "not resolved"), the lab-12 exploitation chain ending in an empty directory, ZAP fuzzing in lab 13 ("it did not fuzz"), tooling install errors in labs 10 and 11, and two lab-02 tasks marked "Did not do." Lab 01's report is effectively screenshots — roughly 400 characters of extractable text across 5 pages, and lab 12 is nearly the same. Two of the fourteen reports carry almost no prose at all. This is a clear instance of documentation-drift: the polished README overstates the completeness of what it indexes. confidence: high — read from the PDFs directly. That the failures are recorded in place at all, rather than quietly dropped, is the habit written up as lab-report-with-working-shown — the drift is in the summary, not in the reports.
    • Some theory answers are visibly filler. Several question blocks are generic, one defers to what a "simple google search shows" (lab 09), and one openly credits an outside video (lab 08). The measured work (entropy tables, timing matrices, histogram comparison) is the strong part; the essay answers are not evidence of understanding and no concept page here cites them as such.
    • Key-material discipline is real and documented. .gitignore excludes *.p12, *.pem, *.key, *.jks and id_rsa*; the README names the private-key screenshot it deliberately did not use; the fixtures are generated rather than committed. See omit-rsa-private-key-screenshot and generate-fixtures-not-commit. The strongest instance of provenance-and-limits-stated-inline in the source set after the Oracle repo.
    • Scope is stated in the repo, not inferred by the wiki.

    > "This is coursework, carried out in 2022 against lab environments and deliberately vulnerable > targets provided for the purpose. ... Nothing here was run against a system without > authorisation, and none of it is written as an operational guide." > — Cybersecurity-Engineering-Laboratories/README.md, 2026-07-25

    • No polish tail. README, LICENSE and .gitignore land together in the final commit df046dc; no badges, no separate license commit. Another counter-example to presentation-polish-tail-commits, while the README remains a readme-as-guided-tour.
    • No .gitattributes and no Git LFS: 14 PDFs and 6 PNGs are committed as plain blobs.

    #Redaction note — this repo is handled differently

    The raw reports contain credentials captured in the MitM lab, ciphertext blocks, an identifier-derived factorisation input, exploit-chain steps and injection payload strings. None of it is reproduced anywhere in this wiki. Every concept page derived from this repo describes the technique class, what it demonstrates and what defence it motivates — and stops there.

    The blanket publish: false was lifted on 2026-07-30; the redaction was not. The two were doing different jobs and the blanket was the weaker of them. Redaction is enforced by what the pages contain — re-read on 2026-07-30 against payload strings, credentials, hashes, CVE identifiers, target addresses and person-like values, and audit-secrets re-checks the published subset on every build. The blanket was enforced by a flag, and its stated reason — keeping §9's "a public page may not link a private one" from forcing a self-contained rewrite that would tempt detail back in — was answered better elsewhere: the renderer emits an unpublished target as plain text, so no page has ever needed rewriting to avoid a dead link.

    What that leaves is the real line, and it is a line about content rather than about a cluster: a page here may name a technique class, a tool's behaviour and the defence it motivates. It may not carry a payload, a captured credential, a target identifier or a working step of an exploit chain. Lab 12's chain is recorded as attempted and partly failed, with no step reproduced. That rule holds whatever the publish flag says.
