    Minimum-Curvature Racing Line

    The fastest path around a circuit is not the shortest one. Cornering speed is capped by grip: v ≤ √(a_lat / κ), where κ is the path's curvature. Reducing curvature raises the speed ceiling everywhere, so the optimal line straightens corners by using the full width of the track — entering wide, clipping the apex, exiting wide — even though the resulting path is longer.

    That gives a two-stage problem: find the low-curvature path within the track corridor, then find the fastest speed profile the car can actually drive along it.

    #Stage 1 — geometry, then relaxation

    racing_line.py builds the centre line by arc-length integration of the track XML — straights, arcs, and clothoid transitions with distinct entry and exit radii (r0/r1). Track geometry comes from the XML rather than the simulator, which is a decision in its own right: parse-track-xml-not-ttrackseg.

    The line is then flattened by periodic Laplacian relaxation inside a corridor of ±(w/2 − 1.5 m) — each node is repeatedly pulled toward the midpoint of its neighbours and clamped back inside the corridor. iters=800, relax=0.2, margin=1.5. It is a smoothing iteration rather than a solve: there is no objective function, no optimality certificate, and no comparison against a published minimum-curvature solver. It is the cheap approximation, and it runs offline once per track.

    #Stage 2 — a physically achievable speed profile

    Curvature gives a friction cap per node, √(a_lat/κ) with mu=1.1 and grip=0.70. That cap is then made reachable by two grade-aware passes over the closed loop:

    • a forward pass limited by a_accel = 5 m/s², so the profile never demands acceleration the car cannot deliver, adjusted by the local gradient;
    • a backward pass limited by a_brake = 8 m/s², so every speed reduction has room to happen before the corner rather than at it.

    Capped at v_top = 55 m/s. At runtime a safe_speed braking envelope reads the profile ahead of the car and returns the highest speed from which it can still make the next constraint.

    #The calibration loop that closes it

    analyze_telemetry.py is the offline race-engineer half: it takes logged laps, computes the p95 measured lateral-g and suggests a --grip value from it, clusters off-track coordinates with a 30 m gap to find real hotspots rather than one-off spins, and audits speed in 50 m bins. Its output feeds back into stage 2 and into the corner memory of residual-reinforcement-learning — measurement replacing a guessed constant.

    #Why this belongs beside the learning

    The line and its speed profile are the prior. They encode everything about the track that is knowable without driving it, computed once, offline, deterministically. Only what remains — tyre behaviour, model error, the specific car's transients — is left for proximal-policy-optimisation to learn. That division is the whole architecture of the agent.
