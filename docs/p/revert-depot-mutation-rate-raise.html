    Revert The Depot-Mutation-Rate Raise

    Decision. Keep p_mut_depot = 0.05. Raise elitism from 2 to 3 instead.

    Date. 2026-07-10 (committed), decided during the P2 modification round on the blind GA in optimization-methods.

    Rejected alternative. p_mut_depot 0.05 → 0.10, tried first and reverted after it regressed every large instance.

    #The reasoning that led into the mistake

    The operator-bake-off had just established that depot mutation is the dominant operator factor — every combination using Random depot mutation beat every combination using Swap. The obvious follow-up is: if depot exploration is the lever that matters, apply more of it. Doubling the depot mutation rate was the data-driven guess.

    It was wrong, and by a lot:

    "That regressed every large instance by 12–17% (berlin best-of-10 1,060,173 → 1,244,639) — at 0.10, ~20 of 200 depot genes reassign per child, destroying good assignments faster than selection can exploit them."

    Optimization Methods/final_project/output/experiments/blind_ga/tuned/mod_note.md, 2026-07-10

    The mechanism is the standard exploration/exploitation failure stated concretely: on a 200-customer instance, 10% depot mutation reassigns roughly 20 genes per child. Selection cannot consolidate a good partition faster than mutation dismantles it. "Which operator matters most" and "how much of that operator to apply" are different questions, and the answer to the first does not imply a direction for the second.

    #The value came from a teammate, and the experiment confirmed it

    The 0.05 base value was not the user's own tuning — it was suggested by a collaborator on the project and adopted as a fixed base for the bake-off, where all numeric parameters were pinned so only operator types varied.

    "Base numeric values are FIXED (friend's tuned winners); only operator TYPES vary."

    Optimization Methods/final_project/mdvrp/include/bakeoff.hpp, 2026-07-10

    The interview confirms the attribution: the p_mut_depot = 0.05 value came from a teammate (interview, 2026-07-28; the collaborator is not named here). The failed raise therefore does double duty — it is a negative result and an independent check that an inherited constant was already near-optimal.

    "confirming the friend's 0.05 was already near-optimal. We reverted and raised elitism instead: a safe, near-neutral change (no regression). Honest takeaway: C19 is already near a local optimum for simple parameter tweaks."

    Optimization Methods/final_project/output/plots/findings.md, 2026-07-10

    #What shipped instead

    elitism 2 → 3. Deliberately modest: higher elitism protects the best individuals, which is what the best-of-10 reporting protocol actually selects on, without disturbing the tuned operator behaviour. Measured as near-neutral — no regression, no dramatic gain. Choosing a change whose worst case is "nothing happens" is the right move once the aggressive change has already cost a full experiment.

    #Why the negative result was kept

    It would have been trivial to ship elitism 2→3 with no mention of the failed attempt. Instead the README's modifications section is titled "Two modifications (one negative, kept honest)", and the regression has its own figure (fig9_blindga_modification.png).

    That is the same instinct as blind-baseline-comparison, where the GA is reported losing to a greedy heuristic in the headline table, and soft-multiplicative-ban-penalty, where a metric artefact is published with its before/after. Across this repo, results that make the work look worse are the ones given figures. See provenance-and-limits-stated-inline and keep-the-superseded-version-in-tree.

    A recorded failed parameter change is also worth more later than a successful one: it marks a direction already searched, which is exactly what nobody writes down.
