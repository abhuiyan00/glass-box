    Data-Driven Gearing over Hand-Tuned RPM Bands

    Decision. Choose gears from the car's own engine data — parsed out of the TORCS car XML — rather than from tuned rpm thresholds or from a learned reward term.

    Date. Shipped in car_spec.py, published 2026-07-10. Third and final answer in a documented three-branch progression that began 2026-05-02.

    #Two alternatives were tried first, and both are recorded

    INDEX.md names the lineage outright:

    "Gear ideas: [gear_strategy_max_jerk] (raw jerk) → [headless] (rpm band) → [a.bhuiyan] (data-driven torque/shift map)."

    IT Applications in Business and Commerce/project/docs/branch-reports/INDEX.md, 2026-07-10

    Attempt 1 — learn it from a jerk reward (gear_strategy_max_jerk, tip 2026-05-02). Gear moved into the action space and the reward paid for rising acceleration, on the theory that a good upshift produces an acceleration spike. Its own report names the flaw: the only acceleration signal available is the difference of successive speedX readings off the UDP sensor, so the jerk estimate is noisy, and jerk_threshold = 1 was "a coarse first guess". The branch stopped at WIP.

    Attempt 2 — hand-tuned rpm band (headless). A reward paying 1.0 inside a 17k–19k rpm "optimum band", tapering outside it, scaled by gear number. It worked well enough to become the branch's headline feature, and calculate_jerk_reward was commented out in its favour — attempt 1 superseded in place. But the constants are guesses fitted to one car:

    "Compared to the data-driven engine model in [[a.bhuiyan]] (car_spec.py parses the real torque curve), headless's rpm bands are hand-tuned constants."

    IT Applications in Business and Commerce/project/docs/branch-reports/headless.md, 2026-07-10

    (The [[a.bhuiyan]] in that line is an Obsidian wikilink in the source report, escaped here so it does not resolve inside this wiki. It refers to the branch, not to a page.)

    That is the rejected alternative stated by the repo itself, in the repo's own words, in the report for the branch that used it.

    #What shipped instead

    "Data-driven car model for TORCS — every number comes from the car's own XML. No hardcoded driveline."

    IT Applications in Business and Commerce/project/code/a.bhuiyan/gym_torcs/car_spec.py, 2026-07-10

    car_spec.py (247 lines) resolves practice.xml → scr_server.xml → the car XML and extracts gear ratios, gear efficiencies, final drive, rear-tyre radius, the rpm→N·m torque curve and the rev limiter. optimal_gear(speed) then returns the gear that maximises wheel tractive force right now, shifting at DEFAULT_SHIFT_FRAC = 0.93 of the limiter. The module's own framing is that this behaves "like a real ECU's shift map" and self-adapts to whatever car is configured.

    It is the same technique as parse-track-xml-not-ttrackseg applied to the vehicle instead of the circuit: read the simulator's configuration rather than guess at its runtime behaviour.

    #Why this ordering matters

    The gearbox stopped being something the agent had to learn. Under residual-reinforcement-learning the deterministic baseline handles gears from the shift map, and PPO only learns a bounded correction on top — so a whole class of reward-shaping term (jerk bonuses, rpm-band bonuses) disappears from the objective. Fewer shaped terms is fewer ways for the policy to find a degenerate optimum.

    car_spec.py also fails loud rather than substituting defaults, so a caller that cannot resolve the car XML falls back deliberately instead of silently driving on invented ratios.

    #Costs and gaps

    • Three branches spent on one subsystem. The gearing thread is named in INDEX.md's "recurring findings" as a persistent problem across the whole team — see branch-per-experiment.
    • Only the a.bhuiyan tree gets the shift map; the shipped stable agent keeps speed-based auto-gear. Two of the repo's own code trees therefore disagree about gearing, and the repo does not reconcile them.
    • TODO: whether DEFAULT_SHIFT_FRAC = 0.93 was derived from the torque curve or chosen by feel is unrecorded, and the interview did not cover it. confidence: low on that constant specifically. Every other number in the module is parsed, not chosen.
