    Logic for Computer Scientists

    Four machines decide the same question — does this follow from that — and the test suite makes them agree. Python 3.12+, standard library only, 127 tests, clean-room from the fifteen-lecture syllabus of the first-semester logic course (INZ001518).

    #Why four

    Soundness and completeness are the theorems that say a truth table, a Gentzen sequent calculus, resolution and SLD resolution must give the same verdict. In the lecture they are proofs about the systems; here they are an assertion that can fail, which is dual-implementation-as-cross-check taken to four implementations that share no code.

    tests/test_proof_systems.py runs sixteen formulas — ten valid, six not — through three of them and asserts agreement formula by formula. The fourth appears in the demo, where the same entailment is a two-clause logic program. A disagreement would not be an interesting result; it would be a bug in one of the four.

    #A failed proof is more useful than a False

    When the sequent calculus cannot close a branch, only atoms remain on it: those on the left must be true and those on the right false. That open branch is a countermodel, and the suite reads the valuation off the failed proof and checks that it falsifies the formula. A prover that returns a bare "not provable" throws that away.

    #Equivalent, equisatisfiable, and the price of each

    Two constructions weaken equivalence to equisatisfiability for the same reason — see equisatisfiable-not-equivalent:

    • Tseitin. CNF by distribution is equivalent and exponential: (a₁ & b₁) | … | (aₙ & bₙ) has exactly 2ⁿ clauses, which the suite asserts as a count rather than a tendency. Naming each subformula with a fresh variable gives a CNF that is linear and merely equisatisfiable — under sixty clauses against 256 at n = 8.
    • Skolemisation. Replacing ∃y by a function of the universally quantified variables in scope removes the existentials and changes the signature, so it cannot be equivalent. It is enough for refutation, which is why every first-order prover starts there.

    #Boundaries in signatures, not in caveats

    Every method here has a point past which it stops working, and each is expressed in the API:

    Module Boundary How it shows up
    truth2ⁿ valuationsMAX_ENUMERABLE_VARIABLES = 20; past it a ValueError naming the row count
    resolutionexponential saturationmax_steps; exceeding it raises, never returns "not refutable"
    prologSLD is incomplete under a fixed clause orderDepthExceeded, distinct from "no solutions"
    predicatefirst-order validity is undecidablethere is no is_valid; is_valid_in takes an explicit family of structures

    "Out of budget" and "no answer" are different facts, and conflating them is how a prover lies.

    #The bug that changed the design

    The grammar module's first membership algorithm searched forward from the start symbol and pruned any sentential form longer than the target. It hung. With S → S S | ε the form S S S S is longer than the target and can still shrink back to it, so nothing was ever prunable. A step cap would have converted the hang into a spurious "not in the language".

    The fix was to transform the input instead: remove epsilon and unit productions first, after which every derivation step either lengthens the form or consumes a nonterminal, and the bounded search becomes a genuine decision procedure. Recorded as transform-the-input-rather-than-cap-the-search.

    #The fifteen lectures, as modules

    Sets, relations, orders and functions (1–3) in relations.py; formal languages and context-free grammars (4) in grammar.py; many-sorted algebras and relational systems (5) in algebra.py; propositional syntax and semantics (6–7) across formula.py, parse.py and truth.py; the Gentzen system (8) in gentzen.py; functional completeness and metalogic (9) in connectives.py; the predicate calculus (10–12) and canonical forms (13) in predicate.py and normal.py; resolution (14) in resolution.py; logic programming (15) in prolog.py.

    Functional completeness is decided twice: Post's criterion reports which of the five closed classes swallows an incomplete set — {&, |} fails because both are monotone and no composition of monotone functions is anything else — and to_nand decides it constructively by rewriting any formula into nands.

    #2026-08-12 reference audit

    Python compilation, 127/127 standard-library tests, and the headless demo passed. The demo confirmed agreement among the truth table, Gentzen, resolution, and SLD resolution for the documented entailment; it also produced the tested normal-form, countermodel, grammar, and relation examples. The existing four-machine visual guide is retained because it follows that reproducible demonstration and states the methods' boundaries.

    #Status and provenance

    Local repository on main, one commit, not pushed. The course reading included a third-party logic textbook; it was not consulted and nothing from it appears here, which NOTICE states explicitly — see provenance-notice-file and clean-room-rebuild-from-specification. TASKS.md lists the intended next steps: DPLL measured against the enumeration, first-order resolution (the unifier and Skolem forms are already here), Chomsky normal form with CYK, and natural deduction beside the sequent calculus.
