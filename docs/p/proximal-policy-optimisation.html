    Proximal Policy Optimisation

    A policy-gradient method that improves a policy by repeatedly taking small, bounded steps away from the policy that collected the data. The bound is what makes it usable: an unconstrained policy-gradient update can move the policy far enough that the trajectories it was fitted on no longer describe it, and the run collapses.

    PPO enforces the bound with a clipped surrogate objective — the probability ratio between the new and old policy is clamped to 1 ± ε, so an update that would move a single action's probability too far stops contributing gradient. Advantages come from GAE (generalised advantage estimation), which trades bias against variance with a single λ.

    #As implemented here

    Both agent trees in it-applications-in-business-and-commerce implement it, twice, with pytorch:

    • Separate actor and critic trunks — two independent MLPs, 256×2 with Tanh activations, rather than the more common shared backbone. The stable tree carries a shape-tolerant checkpoint migration that maps old backbone.* weights onto the split actor/critic layout, which dates the split: a shared backbone came first and was replaced.
    • Target-KL early stopping — target_kl=0.03. Epochs over a batch stop as soon as the measured KL divergence from the collection policy exceeds the threshold, a second bound on top of the clip.
    • init_log_std=-1.2 and entropy_coef=3e-3 — a deliberately narrow initial action distribution with a small entropy bonus holding it open.

    The action space is continuous (steer, throttle, brake), which is why a Gaussian policy with a learned log-standard-deviation is used rather than a categorical one.

    #What PPO does and does not solve here

    It solves credit assignment over a long episode: the agent never receives a "you should have braked 200 m earlier" signal, only per-step reward, and GAE spreads the eventual penalty backwards. It does not solve reward design — see reward-shaping, where six of eight experiment branches are reward experiments and the algorithm is held constant throughout. Nor does it solve the exploration cost of a physics simulator that must be stepped in real time; the parallel branch attacked that with concurrent environments rather than with a different algorithm.

    The residual-reinforcement-learning variant keeps PPO exactly as-is and changes only what the policy's output means — a bounded correction to a deterministic controller rather than the control itself.
