    RSI as Measure, Not Dimension

    Decision. Store the 14-day relative strength index as a nullable numeric column on the fact table dw.FACT_QUOTES, and push the overbought/oversold interpretation into a view. Do not give the indicator its own dimension table.

    Date. 2026-07-25, data-warehousing-and-business-intelligence.

    #Why

    "It is tempting to give an indicator its own dimension table, with the value and a pair of overbought/oversold flags. That inverts the star"

    Data-Warehousing-and-Business-Intelligence/docs/star-schema.md, 2026-07-25

    Two arguments, both about what a dimension is for. A dimension holds the attributes you slice by; a measure is the thing being sliced. RSI14 is numeric, continuous, and changes every day — "nobody slices by a continuous float". See measure-vs-dimension and relative-strength-index.

    The second argument is the more portable one, and it is about thresholds:

    "Storing them freezes one analyst's convention — 70 and 30 — into the warehouse, so moving the threshold would mean rewriting history."

    Data-Warehousing-and-Business-Intelligence/docs/star-schema.md, 2026-07-25

    Overbought and oversold are not properties of a trading day. They are a reading applied to a number, and the number is what the warehouse stores. Materialising the flags would push an analyst's convention into the persistence layer, where changing it means a rewrite rather than a redefinition. dw.vw_QuoteSignals derives both flags in a view, so the convention is one CREATE OR ALTER away from being different.

    #The cost, stated

    "Reached through a dimension keyed by its own surrogate, a day's RSI takes two joins to retrieve a value that could simply have been a column."

    Data-Warehousing-and-Business-Intelligence/docs/star-schema.md, 2026-07-25

    provenance-and-limits-stated-inline — the repo states what the rejected design costs rather than just asserting it is wrong.

    #Alternatives rejected

    Option Why not
    DimRSI keyed by its own surrogate, carrying value + flagsinverts the star; a continuous measure becomes something you slice by; two joins to read one number
    Materialise IsOverbought / IsOversold as fact columnsfreezes 70/30 into stored history; a threshold change becomes a backfill
    Compute the indicator at query time from close pricesnot named in the repo. TODO: whether this was considered is unrecorded — the backfill approach may simply have been the assignment's shape

    #The rejected design is still in the tree

    project/twtr/ssas/Dim RSI.dim exists, with RSIKey, IsOverbought, IsOversold and DateKey attributes, feeding a second cube (Twitter_rsi.cube) whose only measure is a row count. It is exactly the modelling this decision rejects, committed alongside the argument against it. See ssas and keep-the-superseded-version-in-tree.

    Neither DimRSI nor FactRSI is created by any committed DDL, so the cube and the schema do not describe the same database — documentation-drift.

    TODO: whether the cube was built first and the argument written afterwards, or the artefact kept deliberately as a worked counter-example, is unrecorded. confidence: low on the ordering. The decision itself is confidence: high — argued in the design doc, implemented as a fact column in the DDL, and carried through by the view and the backfill script, three independent artefacts agreeing.

    #Generalisation

    The rule this instance teaches is not about indicators. It is: a derived number is a measure; the convention used to read it is not data at all. Anything defined by a cut-off — churn risk bands, SLA breaches, "large" orders — belongs in a view or a semantic layer, not in stored columns, for exactly the reason above. See star-schema and fact-grain.
