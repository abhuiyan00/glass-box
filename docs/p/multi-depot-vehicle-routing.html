    Multi-Depot Vehicle Routing

    Serve every customer from one of several depots, at minimum total travel cost, without exceeding vehicle capacity. The multi-depot variant (MDVRP) adds a second, harder sub-problem on top of ordinary routing: which depot owns which customer. Route order is a permutation problem; the depot partition is an assignment problem, and they interact.

    The instance of it built in optimization-methods makes three choices that move it away from the textbook version and toward something that behaves like a real fleet.

    #The three departures from the textbook

    1. Cost is directed road distance, not Euclidean. Instances are built from real OpenStreetMap drive networks via osmnx, one-way streets respected, so dist(a,b) ≠ dist(b,a) in general. The graph is truncated to its largest strongly-connected component so a shortest-path query never meets an unreachable pair. Distances are resolved by lazy-dijkstra-distance-cache rather than a precomputed matrix.

    2. Capacity is enforced per trip, and vehicles are multi-trip. A vehicle serves, returns to its depot, reloads, and goes out again. Total demand is engineered to be roughly 2–6× a single fleet round, so the multi-trip behaviour is forced rather than incidental. Capacity is drawn per instance from Uniform[40, 70] and is always greater than the maximum demand, so every customer fits in one trip — that keeps infeasibility a property of the plan, not of the instance.

    3. The chromosome carries the partition explicitly. Two aligned rows: a sequence permutation of all customer ids, and a depot_of row assigning each customer a depot. Decoding groups by depot, then greedily splits into capacity-feasible trips, then round-robins trips across that depot's vehicles. Fitness is road distance plus 1e6 × overload units — infeasibility is priced, not forbidden.

    The depot row is the load-bearing gene, and the repo learned that the hard way. It ships an anti-regression assert because an earlier version silently destroyed the search:

    "A prior implementation shipped a sorted depot row, which silently collapses the assignment search; an anti-regression assert at population initialisation fails loudly if the depot gene ever comes out monotonic."

    Optimization Methods/final_project/ARCHITECTURE.md, 2026-07-10

    The operator-bake-off later confirmed the same thing quantitatively: only Random depot mutation can re-partition, Swap merely permutes existing assignments, and every combination using Random beat every combination using Swap.

    #Instance scale

    4 cities (wroclaw berlin zurich budapest) × 3 levels = 12 instances, 5000 m network radius, per-city generation seeds 42–45.

    Level Customers Depots Vehicles/depot
    small5021

    Demand per customer ~ Uniform[1, 20], drawn before capacity so that changing the capacity rule does not resample the demands — a small determinism detail that makes instances comparable across reruns.

    #The geography does not reproduce, and that is documented

    Everything numeric is seeded and reproducible except the map itself:

    "Re-running the deterministic greedy on a freshly downloaded Wrocław graph gave 178,207 / 322,318 / 458,921 m vs the paper's 177,555 / 299,944 / 481,089 m — same demands and capacity, but a different sampled road graph (OSM has drifted; today's largest SCC = 3,893 nodes)."

    Optimization Methods/final_project/docs/DATA_VERIFICATION.md, 2026-07-09

    The frozen paper artefacts (committed reports and chromosomes) are therefore the reference, not the regenerated CSVs — which is why the instance and graph CSVs are gitignored and the reports are not. See osmnx for the drift mechanism and generated-audit-artifact for how the check is published.

    #Related

    Two solvers on the same problem, deliberately kept side by side rather than merged: blind-baseline-comparison (distance only) and workload-balance-vs-distance (LBBS, distance traded for fleet fairness). Both compared against a deterministic nearest-neighbour greedy — multiple-solutions-side-by-side.

    Python and C++ meet only at CSV files here: two-program-csv-contract.
