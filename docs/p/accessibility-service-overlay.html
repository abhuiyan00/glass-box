    Accessibility Service Overlay

    Read another app's live view hierarchy through the platform accessibility channel, compute something from it, then draw the answer back on top of that same app — without modifying it, without root, and without a screenshot. The whole capture architecture of farely rests on this one idea.

    #Why it is a concept and not just an API

    Three properties fall out of the choice, and they are what make it transferable:

    • The text arrives already parsed. Nodes carry text, contentDescription and — with flagReportViewIds — stable view IDs. No image processing, microseconds instead of milliseconds. This is the whole argument in node-tree-read-before-ocr.
    • The overlay is free. A service that already holds the accessibility binding can attach a window of type TYPE_ACCESSIBILITY_OVERLAY with no additional user-granted permission. See accessibility-overlay-over-system-alert-window.
    • The read is total. Everything on screen is legible to the service, including screens the user would never consent to share. That is why the interesting engineering here is restraint, not reach.

    #The restraint half

    Farely narrows the channel at four points before any logic runs:

    1. accessibility_service_config.xml restricts packageNames to four driver apps (ee.mtakso.driver, com.ubercab.driver, taxi.android.driver, com.mytaxi.driver). Nothing else on the phone is observed.
    2. Events are debounced at 300 ms and limited to WINDOW_CONTENT_CHANGED, WINDOW_STATE_CHANGED and NOTIFICATION_STATE_CHANGED.
    3. MultiAppCoordinator.onScreen sees the screen before the parser, and a frozen state short-circuits process() entirely — the identity-check freeze described on farely.
    4. Output is advisory only. The service never dispatches a gesture at a ride offer: never-automate-the-accept-tap.

    The ordering matters: freeze is checked before parse, so a frozen screen is never read, not merely never acted on. The guarantee is still heuristic — it depends on a keyword list recognising the screen — and that limit is recorded rather than smoothed over.

    #Cost of the approach

    • Fragility to redesign. Nodes are an implementation detail of someone else's app. Farely's answer is taught-selectors-over-guessed-labels — let the driver point at the real node instead of guessing labels per locale.
    • Terms-of-service exposure. Reading and overlaying another company's driver app is documented as a ban risk in four separate places in the repo — an instance of privacy-documented-beside-the-feature.
    • User trust cost. Enabling an accessibility service is the single most powerful grant on Android, and the install guide says so.
